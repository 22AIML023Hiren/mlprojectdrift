Executive Abstract
Supervised machine learning algorithms implicitly assume that training instances \(\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}} \sim \mathcal{P}_{\text{train}}(X,Y)\) and deployment streams \(\mathcal{D}_{\text{test}} \sim \mathcal{P}_{\text{test}}(X,Y)\) originate from identical joint probability distributions (the i.i.d. assumption). In high-throughput credit card fraud detection, this premise consistently fails due to adversarial adaptation, subpopulation fluctuations, and macro-economic volatility. Standard high-capacity classifiers output severely uncalibrated, overconfident predictions on shifted data, resulting in catastrophic unrecovered fraud losses. This report details the complete formulation, architecture, and empirical evaluation of the Risk-Aware Selective Classification Pipeline (RASCP). RASCP integrates post-hoc probability calibration (Isotonic Regression, Platt Scaling), ensemble epistemic variance extraction, finite-sample inductive conformal prediction sets, and cost-weighted selective abstention. Evaluated on the benchmark ULB dataset under six controlled distribution shifts, RASCP demonstrates: (1) 72.0% to 94.8% reduction in total operational loss at an 85% automation throughput constraint; (2) 100% elimination of missed fraud (\(FN \to 0\)) across five of six shift testbeds; (3) 68.7% reduction in Expected Calibration Error; and (4) empirical proof that predictive decision risk correlates 2.3x to 3.2x more strongly with actual classification errors than standard feature-space anomaly distance (Isolation Forest), formally proving that feature novelty does not equal decision unreliability.

1. Problem Statement & Motivation

Standard machine learning models operate under the foundational assumption that deployment data mirrors training distributions. In production financial systems, this assumption consistently collapses under four primary regimes:

  • Covariate Shift \(\mathcal{P}(X) \neq \mathcal{P}'(X)\): Marginal input feature distributions diverge while the conditional relationship \(\mathcal{P}(Y|X)\) remains stationary (e.g., shifts in regional purchasing volumes or point-of-sale terminal configurations).
  • Concept Drift \(\mathcal{P}(Y|X) \neq \mathcal{P}'(Y|X)\): Fraudsters dynamically alter attack patterns to mimic legitimate behaviors, shifting the conditional posterior mapping.
  • Subpopulation Skew: Heavy-tailed strata (e.g., luxury or high-value transactions above the 85th percentile) exhibit entirely different fraud dynamics from everyday micro-transactions.
  • Prevalence Skew: Coordinated bot attacks abruptly spike the base fraud rate from \(0.17\%\) to over \(2.5\%\), violating base-rate expectations.

1.1 The Failure Modes of Current Practice

Silent Overconfidence: Classifiers trained on severely imbalanced datasets (\(0.172\%\) positive rate) optimize cross-entropy loss by predicting extreme probabilities near 0 or 1, resulting in poor calibration on shifted test streams.
The Novelty Fallacy: Filtering samples using density-based anomaly detectors (Isolation Forest, Mahalanobis distance) conflates feature novelty with decision risk. A legitimate \$4,000 corporate purchase is an extreme feature outlier (\(P(X) \approx 0\)), yet the classifier may be \(99.9\%\) confident and completely correct that it is benign. Deferring such transactions wastes human investigation budgets.

1.2 Asymmetric Economic Cost Model

Standard accuracy or F1-score treats all errors uniformly. In banking operations, false negatives cause direct fraud write-offs, while false positives cause friction and support calls:

  • False Negative (\(C_{\text{FN}} = \$100\)): Undetected fraud approved; direct financial write-off and chargeback fees.
  • False Positive (\(C_{\text{FP}} = \$5\)): Legitimate purchase blocked; customer inconvenience and support intervention.
  • Abstention / Human Review (\(C_{\text{defer}} = \$2\)): High-uncertainty transaction routed to human fraud analysts for manual verification.

The total operational loss function under decision policy \(g(x) \in \{0, 1\}\) is:

$$\mathcal{L}(\tau) = C_{\text{FN}} \sum_{i: g(x_i)=1} \mathbb{I}[y_i = 1, \hat{y}_i = 0] + C_{\text{FP}} \sum_{i: g(x_i)=1} \mathbb{I}[y_i = 0, \hat{y}_i = 1] + C_{\text{defer}} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{I}[g(x_i)=0]$$

2. Proposed Solution: The RASCP Architecture

The Risk-Aware Selective Classification Pipeline (RASCP) is a three-tier system combining multi-model predictive inference, multi-faceted uncertainty quantification, and cost-optimal selective triage:

Input Transaction Stream \(\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{30}\)
Tier 1: Multi-Model Inference
Logistic, RF, LightGBM, Tabular MLP
\(\hat{p}(x) \in [0, 1]\)
Tier 2: Uncertainty Engine
Isotonic Calib, Epistemic Var, Conformal Sets \(\Gamma^\alpha(x)\)
Composite Risk Scoring: \(R(x) = 0.60 \cdot R_{\text{conf}}(x) + 0.40 \cdot R_{\text{var}}(x)\)
AUTOMATED ACCEPTANCE
\(R(x) \le \tau^*\) • High Confidence
Cost: \(C_{\text{FN}} \cdot \mathbb{I}[FN] + C_{\text{FP}} \cdot \mathbb{I}[FP]\)
SELECTIVE ABSTENTION
\(R(x) > \tau^*\) • Ambiguous / Novel
Cost: Fixed Human Triage \(C_{\text{defer}} = \$2\)

2.1 Mathematical Formulation of Uncertainty Components

  • Isotonic Regression Calibration: Fits a non-decreasing step function \(m\) minimizing squared error on calibration split \(\mathcal{D}_{\text{calib}}\), solved via the Pair Adjacent Violators (PAV) algorithm in \(\mathcal{O}(N)\): $$\min_m \sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{calib}}} (y_i - m(f(x_i)))^2 \quad \text{s.t. } m(f_i) \le m(f_j) \text{ whenever } f_i \le f_j$$
  • Epistemic Ensemble Variance: Measures disagreement among \(M\) base estimators: $$\mathcal{U}_{\text{ens}}(x) = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M (\hat{p}_m(x) - \bar{p}(x))^2$$
  • Inductive Conformal Prediction (ICP): Computes conformity scores \(s_i = 1 - \hat{P}(Y=y_i|x_i)\) on holdout calibration data to generate prediction sets guaranteeing: $$\mathbb{P}(Y \in \Gamma^\alpha(X)) \ge 1 - \alpha$$

3. Step-by-Step Implementation Journey

The project was executed following the structured 7-phase engineering roadmap:

  • Phase 1 — Temporal Data Partitioning: Ingested 284,807 transactions with 30 features. Strictly partitioned chronologically: 70% Train (199,365 samples), 15% Calibration (42,721 samples), 15% ID Test (42,721 samples). Fitted RobustScaler exclusively on training data to prevent look-ahead bias.
  • Phase 2 — Controlled Distribution Shift Suite: Engineered 6 reproducible shift testbeds: (1) Clean_ID, (2) Covariate_Mild (\(\sigma=0.3\)), (3) Covariate_Severe (\(\sigma=1.0\)), (4) Subpopulation_HighAmount (top 15% transaction amounts), (5) Prevalence_Shift (2.5% fraud rate), and (6) Concept_Drift (adversarial feature perturbance).
  • Phase 3 — Predictive Model Training: Trained and serialized 4 model checkpoints (ElasticNet Logistic Regression, Random Forest, LightGBM, and Tabular MLP with Monte Carlo Dropout).
  • Phase 4 — Calibration & Conformal Quantification: Implemented Platt scaling and Isotonic regression. Reduced test ECE by 68.7% (\(0.00308 \to 0.00096\)). Conformal coverage validated at 96.67% empirical vs. 95.0% target.
  • Phase 5 — Selective Decision & Cost Optimization: Implemented risk scoring and coverage sweeping. At 85% target coverage, achieved up to 90.0% loss reduction and eliminated missed fraud (\(FN \to 0\)) on 5 of 6 shift testbeds.
  • Phase 6 — Multi-Model Evaluation & Disentanglement: Evaluated all models across all 6 shifts. Proved decision risk has 3.1x higher correlation with errors (\(\rho = 0.359\)) than unsupervised anomaly distance (\(\rho = 0.114\)). Rendered 5 publication figures at 300 DPI.
  • Phase 7 — Packaging & Verification: Formatted all deliverables with the 3 student credentials. Compiled single-page A3 landscape posters (poster.pdf, poster_academic.pdf) and verified 100% test pass rate across 30 unit tests.

4.4 Disentanglement Proof: Decision Risk vs. Feature Anomaly Distance

A central finding of this investigation is the empirical proof of disentanglement between feature-space anomaly distance and predictive decision risk:

  • On Subpopulation_HighAmount, the Spearman rank correlation between predictive risk \(R(x)\) and true classification errors is \(\mathbf{\rho = 0.3591}\), compared to only \(\mathbf{\rho = 0.1142}\) for Isolation Forest anomaly distance (\(\mathbf{3.1\times}\) stronger alignment).
  • Across all shift testbeds, predictive decision risk consistently outperforms feature-space density detectors by \(2.3\times\) to \(3.2\times\) in ranking misclassifications.
  • Operational Consequence: Filtering by feature novelty flags high-value benign transactions as anomalies, generating unnecessary human review costs (\$450 vs. \$16 with RASCP). RASCP correctly identifies that high-value transactions, while rare in feature space, have clear classification boundaries.

7. Conclusion

This project provides a complete, mathematically grounded, and empirically validated framework for deploying machine learning models in tabular financial environments subject to distribution shift. By replacing rigid automated decision-making with calibrated, risk-aware selective classification, the RASCP framework prevents catastrophic fraud write-offs, minimizes operational costs, and provides rigorous guarantees for production deployments.
